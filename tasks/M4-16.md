the player's `$031D`/`$0321`, and all four are guarded at zero.

**Why the native cannot express it yet.** `ReflectionTransition` carries only
brake, rotate-negative, rotate-positive and jump inputs; there is no A or roll
input for either rider through that path, and `movement.cpp:1893` hardcodes the
roll input to `index==0 && buttons.x`, so the opponent can never roll. Six of
the eight selector values (2-7) therefore have nothing to drive, which is why
the guard fires.

**Why it was never caught.** The branch needs `marker & $2000`, an impulse, a
sloped launch, and `feature_total == 0 || player transitions - opponent
transitions >= 3`. In every captured race the opponent scores event one at frame
1672, so `feature_total` leaves zero almost immediately, and the player never
leads by three transitions at a sloped launch. Measured across `boundary-a`,
`brake-a`, `late-roll-a` and `held-x-a`: the trick selector and both opponent
trick inputs are **0 on all 5,076 race-domain frames of every capture**. No
reference capture exercises this at all, and the existing 6225-state
diagnostics pass precisely because they never reach it.

**What recovery needs.** New original evidence first: a capture whose player
gets three or more transitions ahead of the opponent at a sloped launch, so the
original itself shows what bits 1 and 2 do to the opponent's trajectory. Then
opponent A and roll input plumbing, driving the already-recovered A-reflection
and X-roll mechanics for rider 1, the two guards removed, and fresh independent
review. The player-side mechanics are recovered and validated, so this is
wiring plus evidence rather than new physics — but it is not a small change and
it must not be implemented from the disassembly alone.

Until then M4-16 cannot be accepted: ordinary play can abort mid-race.
